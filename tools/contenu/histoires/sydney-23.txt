--- EN
The idea came to Noah at six in the morning, sitting on his surfboard, waiting for a wave.

He called Manon immediately.

"Page one hundred and eighty-seven," he said, without saying hello.

"What about it?"

"Coralis hid the truth on page one hundred and eighty-seven of a two-hundred-page report. So what if Brightside did the opposite? What if their whole report fitted on one page? Everything. Where their electricity comes from, how much it costs, what's good, what's not good. One page. Anybody can read it in two minutes."

Manon was quiet for a moment. Then she started laughing.

"Noah, that's brilliant."

"I know," said Noah.

They worked on it for two weeks. Tegan sent them all of Brightside's numbers. Manon turned them into simple sentences. Noah designed the page.

At the top, in big letters: "BRIGHTSIDE ENERGY. OUR WHOLE ANNUAL REPORT. ONE PAGE."

Then, simple facts. "Our customers: 4,112 farms, homes and businesses. All of them own a share of the company." "Where your electricity comes from: 81% from solar panels on our members' roofs and sheds. 19% from the national network, which still uses coal. We're not proud of that 19%. Here's our plan to reduce it to zero by 2030." "Our profit last year: $340,000. All of it went back into new batteries." "Our salaries: our best-paid employee earns $95,000. That's me. Tegan."

At the bottom, one line, smaller: "Other energy companies have reports of two hundred pages. Ask them what's on page one hundred and eighty-seven."

Tegan laughed when she saw it.

"Can we really say that? About other companies?"

"It's not illegal to ask a question," said Belinda. "And every number on your page is true."

The poster would appear in newspapers, on buses in Brisbane and on social media. And there would be a short film, too. Tegan's father, Pat, standing in front of his shed full of solar panels, in the red dust of his farm, reading the one page aloud.

"When can we film?" asked Tegan.

"Next week," said Noah. "In Longreach."

« Page cent quatre-vingt-sept, a-t-il dit, sans dire bonjour.

— Coralis a caché la vérité à la page cent quatre-vingt-sept d'un rapport de deux cents pages. Alors si Brightside faisait l'inverse ? Si tout son rapport tenait sur une seule page ? Tout. D'où vient leur électricité, combien elle coûte, ce qui est bien, ce qui ne l'est pas. Une seule page. N'importe qui peut la lire en deux minutes. »

« Noah, c'est génial.
