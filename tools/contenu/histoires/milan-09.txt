On Monday, Chloé showed Aldo the photos and played him the recording of the machines. He listened with his eyes closed.

"We must tell Signora Vittoria," he said. "Today."

"Wait." Chloé sat down. "I've been thinking all night. If we report this, what happens to Hua? She has no papers. The police come, they close the workshop. The boss disappears. And Hua and Lili are sent back to China. With a debt of twelve thousand euros." She put her face in her hands. "I could destroy her life, Aldo. Trying to help."

Aldo was quiet for a long moment.

"You're right to be careful," he said. "But doing nothing is also a choice."

That evening, Sofia called.

"I asked my professor. Her husband is a lawyer. He works with workers' rights. He says there's a law in Italy. If someone is a victim of serious labour exploitation and they help the investigation, they can get a special residence permit. Legal papers. Protection." She took a breath. "Hua wouldn't be sent back. She'd be protected. And Lili too."

"Is that sure?"

"Nothing is sure. But it's possible. The lawyer says workers in Prato have received these permits before, after inspections."

"Can I talk to him?"

"Tomorrow. Seven o'clock. He'll come to the bubble tea shop." Sofia laughed nervously. "His name is Avvocato Bassi. He's sixty and he's never drunk bubble tea in his life."

The next evening, Avvocato Bassi sat in the tiny shop with a large mango bubble tea that he looked at suspiciously.

He listened to everything.

"I've worked in this area for thirty years," he said. "Some workers were helped. Some were deported. The difference is always the same." He looked at Chloé. "It depends on who arrives first. If the police arrive first, without preparation, it's a disaster. If the lawyers, the inspectors and the social services arrive together, with a plan, the workers are protected."

"So we need a plan."

"We need a plan," said Avvocato Bassi. He tried the bubble tea and made a face. "And something better to drink."
