--- EN
Inès came back to London on Monday night with copies of everything Claire had given her. On the train, she wrote everything down in her notebook.

Step one: Swift Freight takes Verrier boxes from Dartford. The system says SHIPPED.

Step two: the truck does not go to Dover. It goes to Unit 14 in Essex, where the glass is sold cheaply to a discount shop.

Step three: the client complains. Harper & Lane pays Swift Freight a second time, at a high price, for an urgent delivery.

Step four: someone in finance calls Maison Verrier and asks them to pay "special handling fees".

Step five: Richard Hale signs every payment.

But one question was still missing. Why would a finance director take such a risk? Richard earned a very good salary. He had a big house, an expensive car, a place on the board.

On Tuesday, Inès met Helen Park again at Café Rosa. She showed her the forms from Lyon. Helen read them for a long time.

"This is the proof I needed," she said. "A full signature, on documents sent to a client, for fees that don't exist in any contract. And now I can tell you what I found."

She opened her laptop and turned it towards Inès. It was a page from the official register of companies.

"Swift Freight Limited. Created four years ago. The sales director is Mark Bell. But look at the owners."

There were two names. The first was Mark Bell. The second was Susan Hale.

"Susan Hale," said Inès slowly.

"Richard Hale's wife," said Helen. "She owns fifty percent of Swift Freight. Every pound that Harper & Lane paid to Swift Freight, half of it went into the Hale family's pocket."

Inès sat back. For a moment, she could not speak.

"What happens now?"

"Now I write my report. It will take a few days. And I give it to the board." Helen closed the laptop. "But be careful, Inès. Richard doesn't know what we have. Not yet. And men like Richard become very dangerous when they understand that they are losing."
