--- EN
Claire Verrier did not leave. Ten days later, she came to London.

She arrived at Harper & Lane at ten in the morning, in a long grey coat, with a small wooden box under her arm. Priya gave her a visitor badge and her best smile. Margaret Lane came down from the top floor to meet her. That had never happened before.

The meeting was short. The new contract was ready. Inès had written it with Daniel and the lawyers, and Claire had read every line twice in Lyon. Three years. A new transport partner, NorthLine, large and reliable. A weekly report on every shipment. A direct phone number for Inès. And one new rule, written in bold: no fees of any kind could be added without the written agreement of both parties.

Claire signed. Margaret signed. Everyone shook hands.

"Twelve years," said Claire, "and this is the first contract I sign with pleasure."

In the evening, there was a small dinner in an Italian restaurant near the river. Margaret, Daniel, Tom and Jess, Priya, Helen Park and Claire. Inès had also invited one more person. He arrived late, in his best jacket, a little uncomfortable.

"Ray!" said Inès. "You came."

"Couldn't say no to free food, love," said Ray. But he was smiling.

Claire stood up and opened her wooden box. Inside, there were eight small glass birds, blue like the logo of Maison Verrier. She gave one to each person at the table.

"In my family," she said, "we give a glass bird to people who protect what is fragile. This one is for an old man with a notebook. This one is for a young man who made copies. This one is for an auditor who believed a stranger." She gave the last bird to Inès. "And this one is for a young woman who kept her promise. By Friday."

Everybody laughed and raised their glasses.

Later, when the dessert arrived, Laura Kim appeared at the door of the restaurant.

"I'm sorry to interrupt," she said. "Inès, can I talk to you for five minutes?"
