The next morning, Sami showed his table to Fiona. She studied it in silence for ten minutes. Then she took off her glasses.

"Forty thousand pounds," she said. "And I told Rosa the computer was old."

"You couldn't know," said Sami.

"It's my job to know." Fiona stood up and walked to the window. "Before we do anything, I need to be sure. Gregor has worked here for fifteen years. A table is not proof. Rosa's team could make mistakes too."

"Then we need to see it happen," said Sami.

Fiona nodded slowly. Then she turned around with a strange expression.

"There's something else. I received a call yesterday from a friend in the hotel business. The Thistle Guide is sending an inspector to Edinburgh this summer. Secretly, as a normal guest. They will visit about ten hotels. Probably us."

The Thistle Guide was the most important hotel guide in Scotland. A good review could fill a hotel for a year. A bad review could destroy it.

"Does Mr Crane know?" asked Sami.

"I told him last night," said Fiona. "And do you know what he said?"

Sami waited.

"He said, 'Good. Let them see the truth.'" Fiona shook her head. "Any other owner would panic. He sounded almost happy."

"Maybe he wants a bad review," said Sami slowly. "If the hotel looks bad, it's easier to sell."

"Or cheaper," said Fiona.

They looked at each other. It was a terrible idea. An owner who wanted his own hotel to fail.

"I don't want to believe it," said Fiona.

"Neither do I," said Sami. "But let's watch the nights first. Then we'll know more."

Fiona picked up the phone. "I'll change the schedule. Next weekend, you work the night with Gregor. Officially, for training."

"And unofficially?"

"Unofficially," said Fiona, "you keep your eyes open."
