--- EN
The next morning, at half past eight, Rosalind Yeo arrived at Harbourline as usual. Her badge did not open the door.

Two security guards were waiting for her in the lobby, next to the aquarium with the sharks. A man from human resources gave her a letter. She was dismissed immediately "for stealing confidential company data." Her personal things were already in a cardboard box.

The guards walked her out of the building in front of everyone.

Nadia heard about it at ten o'clock. She ran out of the Carrow Whitfield tower and took a taxi.

She found Rosalind sitting at a bus stop near Harbourline's building, with her cardboard box on her knees. A photo of her late husband was on top.

Rosalind looked up. Her face was calm, but her eyes were red.

"They knew," she said simply. "Someone told them. Or they guessed."

Nadia sat down next to her. "I'm so sorry, Rosalind."

"Don't be sorry. I knew this could happen." Rosalind touched the photo of her husband. "He always said I was too honest for business. He was right."

"Listen to me." Nadia took her hand. "You're not alone. The firm promised you a lawyer. I'm calling her now."

The lawyer, Mrs Siti Rahman, arrived forty minutes later in a small red car. She sat on the bench next to Rosalind and read the dismissal letter.

"'Stealing confidential data,'" she read aloud, and laughed. "Mrs Yeo, you gave evidence of a serious crime to the company's own auditors. That's not stealing. That's being a good citizen." She folded the letter. "Dismissing an employee for reporting fraud is illegal. We'll fight this, and we'll win."

"And my mother? My salary?"

"You'll be compensated. And Carrow Whitfield will cover your costs until then. Dr Rao already confirmed it." Siti Rahman stood up and picked up the cardboard box. "Now come. I'll drive you home. You've done enough for one day."

Rosalind looked back once at the tall tower of Harbourline, shining in the sun.

"Eight years," she said quietly. Then she got into the car.

Le lendemain matin, à huit heures et demie, Rosalind Yeo est arrivée chez Harbourline comme d'habitude. Son badge n'ouvrait pas la porte.

Deux agents de sécurité l'attendaient dans le hall, à côté de l'aquarium aux requins. Un homme des ressources humaines lui a remis une lettre. Elle était licenciée avec effet immédiat « pour vol de données confidentielles de l'entreprise ». Ses affaires personnelles étaient déjà dans un carton.

Les agents l'ont escortée hors du bâtiment devant tout le monde.

Nadia l'a appris à dix heures. Elle est sortie en courant de la tour de Carrow Whitfield et a pris un taxi.

Elle a trouvé Rosalind assise à un arrêt de bus près de l'immeuble de Harbourline, son carton sur les genoux. Une photo de son défunt mari était posée dessus.

Rosalind a levé les yeux. Son visage était calme, mais ses yeux étaient rouges.

« Ils savaient, a-t-elle dit simplement. Quelqu'un le leur a dit. Ou ils ont deviné. »

Nadia s'est assise à côté d'elle. « Je suis tellement désolée, Rosalind.
