--- EN
Carrow Whitfield's offices were on the thirty-eighth floor of a glass tower in Marina Bay. From the windows, Nadia could see hundreds of ships waiting in the sea, like a city floating on the water.

"That's the busiest port in the world," said a woman behind her.

Mei Lin Ong was about thirty-five, small, with short black hair and a phone in each hand.

"Welcome! You survived the rain? Good. Sit down, we have a lot to do." She put one phone down. "My twins. Five years old. They want to know if you're a real French person. I said yes. They want you to say 'croissant.'"

Nadia laughed and said "croissant" into the phone.

Then Mei Lin became serious. She introduced the third member of the team: Arjun Pillai, twenty-four, tall, with glasses and a laptop covered in programming stickers.

"Arjun is our data boy. Give him a million numbers, and in ten minutes he finds the strange one."

"Hello," said Arjun shyly. "I like strange numbers."

Mei Lin opened the Harbourline file on the big screen.

"Our job is simple to explain and difficult to do. Harbourline says it made one billion two hundred million dollars in sales last year. We must check that it's true. We take samples. We ask customers and banks to confirm. We look at contracts, ships, warehouses."

"And the deadline?" asked Nadia.

"The twenty-eighth of February. Harbourline wants to raise five hundred million dollars from investors in March. Investors won't give money without our signature on the accounts." Mei Lin looked at both of them. "So everybody wants us to finish fast and say everything is fine."

"And if everything is not fine?"

Mei Lin was quiet for a moment.

"Then we say it's not fine. That's the whole point of our job." She smiled again. "But don't worry. Harbourline has been our client for six years. Every year, the audit was clean. Desmond, our partner, says they're the best-run company in Asia."

Arjun looked at his screen. He did not say anything. But he wrote something in his notebook.
