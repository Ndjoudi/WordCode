--- EN
Ethan did not go home that night. Neither did Yanis.

"One test proves nothing," said Ethan. "It could be a strange accident. We need hundreds."

He wrote a small program. It took real CVs from the Lakeshore applications and made pairs. In each pair, the two CVs were identical except for one detail. A Canadian name or a foreign name. Training in Canada or abroad. A gap of one year in the career, or no gap. A date of birth in 1995 or in 1970.

Five hundred pairs. TalentLens scored them all in four minutes.

At three in the morning, Ethan turned his screen towards Yanis. His voice was very quiet.

"Foreign training: minus forty-five points on average. A foreign-sounding name, even with Canadian training: minus eighteen. A gap of one year, usually a woman who had a baby: minus twenty-five. Over fifty years old: minus thirty."

Yanis looked at the numbers for a long time.

"How is that possible? You never told it to do that."

"No. I told it to learn from the past." Ethan rubbed his eyes. "I trained it on ten years of hiring decisions from Sterling Reed's clients. Who was hired, who was promoted, who stayed. It found patterns. Most of the people hired in the past were young, Canadian-trained, with Canadian names and no gaps. So it learned that those things mean 'good candidate.'"

"So it copied every prejudice of the last ten years."

"And made them invisible." Ethan laughed bitterly. "When a human manager rejects a nurse because of her name, you can see it, you can challenge it. When a machine gives her thirty-four points, everybody thinks it's mathematics."

"In Ontario," said Yanis slowly, "it's illegal to discriminate against job applicants because of their place of origin, their age or their family status. It's in the Human Rights Code."

"I know," said Ethan.

"Then we have to tell Bradley."

Ethan looked at him for a long time.

"You haven't met Bradley when somebody tells him something he doesn't want to hear," he said.

Un nom canadien ou un nom étranger. Une formation au Canada ou à l'étranger. Une interruption d'un an dans la carrière, ou pas d'interruption. Une date de naissance en 1995 ou en 1970.

« Formation à l'étranger : moins quarante-cinq points en moyenne. Un nom à consonance étrangère, même avec une formation canadienne : moins dix-huit. Une interruption d'un an, en général une femme qui a eu un bébé : moins vingt-cinq. Plus de cinquante ans : moins trente. »
