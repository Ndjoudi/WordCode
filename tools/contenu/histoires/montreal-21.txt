--- EN
One week after the ice storm, the electricity came back everywhere in Montreal. Life returned to normal. In the emergency department, the nights were busy again, but ordinary.

During the magic half hour, Marcel sat with Antoine in the staff room. He did not eat his sandwich. He turned his coffee cup in his hands.

"I need to tell you something," he said. "I've never told anyone at work. Only Lucie knows. And Dr Clarke, probably. She knows everything."

Antoine waited.

"Twenty-two years ago, I drank. A lot. Every night after my shift. Then before my shift too. I hid bottles in my locker. I came to work smelling of mints, to hide the smell. I thought nobody noticed."

"Marcel..."

"Somebody noticed. The old night chief, Dr Moreau. He took me into his office one morning. I was sure he would fire me. I deserved it." Marcel smiled sadly. "Instead, he said, 'Marcel, you are the best nurse in this department. And you are killing yourself. I won't watch that happen.' He called my wife. He found me a place in a treatment centre. He kept my job for me for three months."

"And after?"

"After, I came back. I haven't had a drink for twenty-two years." He put down his cup. "That's why I gave you the green notebook. Not to catch Kevin. I suspected someone was sick. I wanted somebody to save him, like Moreau saved me."

Antoine was silent for a long moment.

"Why didn't you watch him yourself?"

Marcel looked at the floor. "Because I was afraid I would see myself. Old men are cowards sometimes." He laughed softly. "You were braver than me, Frenchman."

The radio crackled. An ambulance was coming. A man with chest pain.

Marcel stood up and put on his glasses.

"Come on," he said. "Grey face, sweat, a man who doesn't complain. What level?"

"Two," said Antoine.

"Good," said Marcel. "You're learning."
