The day after Gary Dunn's arrest, Léa received a message from a number she knew.

"The Crown. Saturday. Same time. I'm buying."

The old pub in Stockport looked exactly the same: the fire, the dark wood, the quiet corners. But Kelly looked different. She was sitting by the window this time, not in the darkest corner. She was smiling.

"I've got a new job," she said, before Léa even sat down. "Fundraising manager. For a hospice in Stockport. A real charity. I checked their accounts before the interview." She laughed. "Seventy-eight percent goes to patient care. I asked them for the numbers, and they were happy to show me."

"Kelly, that's wonderful."

"They asked me in the interview why I left Little Stars." Kelly turned her glass on the table. "For the first time, I told the truth. I said I asked questions about the money, and they fired me. The director said, 'That's exactly the kind of person we want.'"

They ate fish and chips. They talked about everything except Little Stars.

Then Kelly became serious.

"Nobody knows it was me," she said. "Not my family, not my friends, not the police. When the officers interviewed former employees, they asked if anyone had kept documents. I said I didn't know. Nobody ever asked me about you." She looked at Léa. "You kept your promise."

"Of course."

"It's not 'of course.' Most people don't." Kelly finished her wine. "I was so scared that night, the first time we met. I almost didn't come. I sat in my car outside for twenty minutes."

"Why did you come in?"

Kelly thought about it.

"Because of the little boy. The one who asked me about the games console." She smiled sadly. "The hospital told me last week that he's in remission. He's going back to school in January. When the new family room opens, I'm going to bring him a games console. The best one. With my own money."

Léa raised her glass. "To the little boy."

"To the little boy," said Kelly.
