--- EN
Aylin arrived at the office twenty minutes later, with her coat over her pyjamas and her hair in a messy knot. She listened to Camille without a word.

When Camille finished, Aylin sat down on the floor next to Bruno's cushion. The dog put his head on her knee.

"Nine years," she said finally. "And he didn't tell me he wanted to sell my company."

"I'm sorry," said Camille.

"Don't be. It's not your fault." Aylin stroked the dog's ears for a long time. "You know what hurts most? Not the sale. People sell companies. What hurts is that he used our clients to do it. Lukas, Mia, all of them. They trusted us."

Camille sat down on the floor too. The office was dark except for one lamp.

"What do you want to do?" she asked.

"What is right," said Aylin. "But first I need to understand what is right."

They talked for an hour. They discussed every option. They could confront Jonas tomorrow. They could tell Margaret. They could talk to a lawyer. They could tell the clients. They could do nothing and hope the problem disappeared.

"Doing nothing is not an option," said Aylin. "The data is out. Planwise has it. Maybe others too."

"Then the first priority is the clients," said Camille. "Not Jonas, not Planwise, not the investment."

Aylin nodded slowly. "And the law. In Europe, when personal data leaks, a company must tell the authorities quickly. I don't remember the exact rules. We need a lawyer tomorrow morning."

"Do you know one?"

"Frau Klein. She helped us write our contracts when we started. She's tough, and she doesn't like Jonas." For the first time that evening, Aylin almost smiled. "That's useful."

At midnight, Aylin stood up.

"Tomorrow, we secure everything. Then we see the lawyer. Then we talk to Jonas. In that order."

"And if Jonas tries to stop us?"

Aylin put on her coat. "Then he will have to remember who built the system. I have all the keys, Camille. I always had them."

« Neuf ans, a-t-elle fini par dire. Et il ne m'a pas dit qu'il voulait vendre mon entreprise.

Camille s'est assise par terre elle aussi. Le bureau était sombre, à part une lampe.

« Qu'est-ce que tu veux faire ? a-t-elle demandé.

— Ce qui est juste, a répondu Aylin. Mais d'abord, j'ai besoin de comprendre ce qui est juste. »

Elles ont parlé pendant une heure. Elles ont examiné toutes les options. Elles pouvaient confronter Jonas le lendemain. Elles pouvaient prévenir Margaret. Elles pouvaient consulter un avocat. Elles pouvaient prévenir les clients. Elles pouvaient ne rien faire et espérer que le problème disparaisse.

« Ne rien faire, ce n'est pas une option, a dit Aylin. Les données sont dehors. Planwise les a. D'autres aussi, peut-être.

Aylin a hoché lentement la tête. « Et la loi. En Europe, quand des données personnelles fuient, une entreprise doit prévenir les autorités rapidement. Je ne me souviens plus des règles exactes. Il nous faut une avocate demain matin.
