--- EN
In December, Angus Morrison invited Hugo to his house. Nobody on the site could believe it.

Angus lived alone in a small stone cottage near the coast. Inside, there was one big room with a peat fire burning in the fireplace.

Angus poured two glasses of whisky without asking.

"You've been looking at my moor," he said. "Every morning. At that machine near the road. Number seven."

Hugo was surprised. "How do you know?"

"I walk the dog at eight." Angus sat down by the fire. "A man doesn't look at something like that every morning unless he's worried."

Hugo hesitated. Then, slowly, he told him. The cracks inside the blades. Jens. Duncan's decision. The four hundred and ten metres. The school bus.

Angus listened without moving. When Hugo finished, he was quiet for a long time, looking at the fire.

"My brother Donald was a fisherman," he said finally. "In January 1968, there was a storm. The worst in a hundred years. The radio said it would pass to the north. The old men in the harbour said no, it's coming here. The sea is the wrong colour. The geese are flying low." He drank his whisky. "Donald listened to the radio. His boat never came back."

"I'm sorry."

"It was a long time ago." Angus stood up and went to the window. Outside, in the grey evening light, a long line of grey geese was flying low over the sea, very low, almost touching the waves.

"Look at them," said Angus. "When they fly like that in December, a big storm is coming. Not this week. Maybe in three or four weeks. After the new year." He turned round. "A really big one, Frenchman. The kind we get every twenty years."

"The forecast doesn't say anything."

"The forecast only looks five days ahead." Angus sat down again. "The geese look further."

"What will you do?" he asked.

Hugo looked at the fire for a long time.

"I don't know yet," he said honestly.

"Then find out," said Angus. "Before the geese are right."

En décembre, Angus Morrison a invité Hugo chez lui. Personne sur le chantier n'arrivait à y croire.

Angus vivait seul dans une petite maison en pierre près de la côte. À l'intérieur, il y avait une grande pièce avec un feu de tourbe qui brûlait dans la cheminée.

Angus a servi deux verres de whisky sans demander.

« Vous regardez ma lande, a-t-il dit. Tous les matins. Cette machine près de la route. La numéro sept. »

Hugo a été surpris. « Comment vous le savez ?

— Je promène le chien à huit heures. » Angus s'est assis près du feu. « Un homme ne regarde pas une chose comme ça tous les matins s'il n'est pas inquiet. »

Hugo a hésité. Puis, lentement, il lui a raconté. Les fissures à l'intérieur des pales. Jens. La décision de Duncan. Les quatre cent dix mètres. Le bus scolaire.

« Mon frère Donald était pêcheur, a-t-il fini par dire. En janvier 1968, il y a eu une tempête. La pire depuis cent ans. La radio disait qu'elle passerait au nord. Les vieux du port disaient non, elle vient ici. La mer n'a pas la bonne couleur. Les oies volent bas. » Il a bu son whisky. « Donald a écouté la radio. Son bateau n'est jamais revenu.
