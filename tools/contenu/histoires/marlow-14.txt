"He's a director of Stayzy Scotland," said Sami. "He's buying his own hotel."

"From himself," said Fiona. "At a low price, after three years of making it look poor."

She stood up and walked around the office. Sami had never seen her like this. Her hands were moving, and her face was red.

"Thirty years," she said. "I've worked for this family for thirty years. I worked for his father. A good man. He would be ashamed."

"But if Douglas owns the hotel," said Sami, "is it even illegal? It's his hotel."

Fiona stopped. Then, slowly, she smiled for the first time that morning.

"That," she said, "is the most important question. And the answer is no. It's not only his hotel."

She opened a cupboard and took out an old folder. Inside, there was a document with a red seal.

"When old Mr Crane died, he left the hotel to his two children. Fifty percent to Douglas. Fifty percent to his daughter, Isobel."

"His sister owns half?"

"Half. But she lives in the Highlands, in a small village near Ullapool. She's a doctor there. She never comes to Edinburgh. She trusts her brother completely. He sends her the numbers every year, and every year the numbers are worse."

Sami understood. "So if Douglas sells the hotel cheaply to a company he partly owns, his sister loses money."

"Half of a cheap price," said Fiona. "While Douglas gets half of a cheap price, plus a share of a good hotel, plus three years of cash in his pocket."

"Does Isobel need to agree to the sale?"

"Yes. Both owners must sign." Fiona closed the folder. "She will probably sign whatever her brother sends her. Unless someone tells her the truth first."

"Then someone must tell her," said Sami.

Fiona looked at the calendar on the wall. "The owners must sign the sale on the last day of August. But Douglas can send her the papers before that, by post."

She picked up her car keys. "How do you feel about a trip to the Highlands, Mr Benali?"
