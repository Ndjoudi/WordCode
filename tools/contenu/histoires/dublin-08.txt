--- EN
Julie did not sleep well. At seven the next morning, she was at her desk. She opened the firm's file system and found Siobhán Kelly's file.

At the top of the page, there was a small grey button: "File history." She clicked on it.

The list was short. "File opened: 3 September, by C. Walsh. Conflict check: completed, 3 September, by C. Walsh. Result: no conflict. File assigned to: J. Martin."

She read it again. Conor had opened the file himself. He had done the conflict check himself. And he had written "no conflict," when he was the partner responsible for MedAxis's parent company.

Declan arrived at eight with two cups of tea. He saw her face and closed the door.

"What's wrong?"

She showed him the screen. Declan read it, and his usual smile disappeared.

"Ah," he said. "That's not good."

"Is it possible that he didn't know? That he forgot MDX owns MedAxis?"

Declan sat down on the edge of her desk. "Julie, Conor Walsh plays golf with the chief executive of MDX every Saturday. He went to his daughter's wedding. He knows."

"Then why give me the case? Why not just refuse it?"

Declan thought for a moment. "If the firm refuses Siobhán, she goes to another firm. Maybe a firm that fights. A firm that goes to court, finds evidence, talks to journalists."

Julie understood slowly. "But if the firm accepts her case and gives it to a junior lawyer in her first month..."

"...then the junior lawyer is nervous, does what the senior partner says, and advises the client to take the money and sign the confidentiality clause. Quick, clean, quiet."

Julie remembered Conor's words in the pub. Small cases are often the best. Quick, clean, a good settlement.

"He chose me because he thought I would be easy," she said.

"Yes," said Declan. He drank his tea. "So the question is: are you?"

Julie looked at the screen for a long time.

"No," she said.
