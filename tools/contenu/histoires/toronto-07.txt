Bradley Sterling listened to them for exactly four minutes. Then he held up his hand.

"Stop. I understand." He smiled his perfect smile. "You've found that the program prefers candidates with Canadian experience. Of course it does. The program is just being realistic."

"It's not realistic," said Yanis. "It's illegal. Place of origin, age, family status. The Ontario Human Rights Code..."

"Yanis." Bradley's voice was still friendly, but his eyes were not. "TalentLens doesn't reject anybody. It gives scores. The client decides."

"They only interview the top three hundred because we told them those are the best."

"And they are the best, according to the data." Bradley turned to Ethan. "Ethan, you're a brilliant engineer. Maybe there's a small statistical issue. Fix it quietly, in the next version. In six months. No need to make a drama."

"In six months," said Ethan, "Lakeshore will have hired one hundred and fifty nurses from a list where foreign-trained nurses had almost no chance."

"Then Lakeshore will have one hundred and fifty excellent nurses." Bradley stood up. "Listen, both of you. TalentLens is the future of this firm. Next month, we're presenting it to three banks. Millions of dollars. I won't let a few test results destroy that."

"Yanis, send Lakeshore the list on Friday, as promised." He paused. "And remember, your work permit is connected to your job here. I'd hate for anything to complicate that."

Yanis felt the blood leave his face.

In Canada, his work permit allowed him to work for one employer: Sterling Reed. If he lost his job, he could lose his right to stay in the country. Bradley knew it.

In the corridor, Ethan said quietly, "He actually threatened you."

Yanis looked down at the frozen city. Eighteen months ago, he had been down there in the snow, on a bicycle, delivering pizza to people in towers like this one.

He did not want to go back down.

But he could not stop thinking about a nurse who had been rejected in nine minutes.

Yanis a senti le sang quitter son visage.
