Laura Kim and Inès walked along the river. The night was cold and clear. On the other side of the water, the lights of the city were reflected in long gold lines.

"Atlas has finished its checks," said Laura. "We are going ahead with the acquisition. The deal will be signed next week. Without the problems you found, we would have paid millions for a company with a hole in its accounts. My boss in New York wants to know who you are."

"I only did my job."

"Most people don't. That's the point." Laura stopped and looked at her. "So here is my offer. After the merger, Atlas needs a head of client accounts for Europe. Someone who speaks two languages, who understands clients, and who is not afraid of difficult questions. We would like it to be you."

Inès was silent. A boat passed under the bridge, with music and laughter.

"What about Daniel?"

"Daniel has asked to take a different role. Training new managers. He says it is time for him to teach, not to lead." Laura smiled. "He was the first person to recommend you."

"And the Verrier account?"

"That's your decision. It will be in your team."

Inès thought of Tom, who needed security for his family. Tom, who had come back when it mattered.

"I want Tom to manage it," she said. "He knows the warehouse, he knows the drivers, and Claire likes him."

"Then it's done." Laura held out her hand. "Welcome to Atlas, Inès."

Three weeks later, on a grey Monday morning, Inès arrived at the office at eight. She had a new desk, near the window, and a small blue glass bird next to her computer.

There was a folder on her desk. On the cover, someone had written a name she did not know: a wine producer in Porto. Inside, there was a short letter. Late deliveries. Missing boxes. An angry client.

Inès smiled, took off her coat, and opened the folder.
