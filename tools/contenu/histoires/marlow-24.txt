Fiona put the phone on speaker so Sami could hear.

"Fiona." Douglas's voice was cold. "I'm looking at the August report. One hundred percent. Explain."

"It's the Festival, Douglas. Every hotel in Edinburgh is full in August."

"Last August we were at sixty-two percent."

"Yes," said Fiona calmly. "Isn't that strange?"

There was a silence on the line. Sami could almost hear Douglas thinking.

"Is Gregor still working nights?"

"Of course. Every night. He's doing an excellent job."

Another silence. "Good. Listen, the meeting with Stayzy is on the thirty-first. Their lawyers and mine will be in the hotel at ten o'clock. You will prepare the big meeting room, coffee, the usual. My sister will sign by post. She always does."

"Of course, Douglas."

He hung up without saying goodbye.

Fiona put down the phone and looked at Sami. "He's nervous. He's never asked about Gregor before."

That evening, Isobel called from the Highlands. Sami told her about the call.

"He sent me the papers yesterday," she said. "The contract with Stayzy, ready to sign. Two and a half million. With a little note: 'Dear Izzy, just sign on page twelve and send it back. Nothing to worry about. Love, Douglas.'"

"What will you do?" asked Sami.

"I won't send anything." Her voice was firm. "I'll come to the meeting in person. Douglas doesn't know. I'll take the first train on the thirty-first and arrive at a quarter to ten."

"He'll be very surprised."

"He'll be very angry. Douglas has never liked surprises. When we were children, he hated birthday parties for that reason." Isobel laughed sadly. "Sami, can you ask Mrs Reid to come too? And Gregor?"

"Gregor finishes his last night shift at seven that morning."

"Then ask him to stay a few hours more," said Isobel. "This time, he won't be working for my brother. He'll be working for the truth."
