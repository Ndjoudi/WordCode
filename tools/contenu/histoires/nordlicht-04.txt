--- EN
Jonas did not answer the email. The next morning, he was in a meeting all day. Aylin replied with one word: "Yes."

On Friday, Camille decided to leave the office. She wanted to see the app in real life. She took the train to a restaurant called Brot & Butter, one of Nordlicht's first clients.

The owner, Lukas, was a big man with a red beard and flour on his hands. He was busy, but he made her a coffee.

"So you're from Nordlicht," he said. "Do you want to know the truth about your app?"

"Yes," said Camille. "That's why I'm here."

"I love it," said Lukas. "Before Shiftly, I spent four hours every week on the schedule. Now I spend thirty minutes. That's the good part."

"And the bad part?"

Lukas called a young waitress. "Mia, tell her about Sunday."

Mia looked a little shy. "Last month, Lukas changed my shift from Sunday morning to Sunday evening. The app sent a message, but I didn't see it. I turned off the notifications because the app sends too many. I came at nine in the morning. The restaurant was closed."

"And in the evening," said Lukas, "we had a big table of twenty people and only one waiter."

Camille wrote quickly in her notebook. "How many messages does the app send you?"

Mia showed her phone. There were eleven messages from Shiftly that day. "Your shift starts in two hours." "Remember your break." "A colleague wants to swap." "Your schedule is updated." Most of them were not important.

"I only want to know when something changes," said Mia. "Not every small thing."

Camille thanked them both. On the train back, she looked at the photos she had taken of Mia's phone. She felt that she was right. The new design and the chat could wait. The notifications could not.

When she arrived at the office, Jonas was waiting for her.

"I read your email," he said. His smile was gone. "We need to talk."
