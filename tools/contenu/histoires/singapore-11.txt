"At Harbourline, there are two sets of accounts. The official one, which you see. And another one, on a separate computer server in Grace Lim's office. Inside the company, it's called 'Project Lion.'"

"What's in it?" asked Nadia.

"The truth." Rosalind turned her cup. "Every month, I prepare the real numbers. Then I send them to Grace. Two days later, the official numbers come back. Always bigger. Sales multiplied by three."

"Orchid Star?"

"Orchid Star. Silver Bay Trading. Eastern Pearl Logistics. Seven companies like that. All created by Grace's people. All in Labuan or the Virgin Islands." Rosalind looked at Nadia. "The real Harbourline is a small, honest shipping company with three old ships. It makes about four hundred million dollars a year. Not one billion two hundred."

Nadia felt cold, even in the warm morning air.

"And the money from investors? The five hundred million in March?"

"Harbourline owes a lot of money to banks. Old loans. Jonathan needs the new money to pay the old debts. Then, next year, he'll need more money to pay those debts." Rosalind shook her head. "It's like a man running faster and faster to stay in the same place."

"Why haven't you gone to the police?"

Rosalind was quiet for a long time.

"I'm a widow. I take care of my mother. She's eighty-nine. If I lose my job, I lose everything. And Jonathan Teo is a very powerful man. He has friends everywhere." She looked at her hands. "I have a copy of Project Lion. On a USB key. At home. I made it two years ago, when I couldn't sleep anymore. But I'll give it to you only when I know you're protected. When I know your firm will stand behind you."

"I understand."

Rosalind stood up and left five dollars on the table.

"One more thing," she said. "In the official accounts, there is a warehouse in Batam, in Indonesia. Full of machinery. Eighty million dollars." She almost smiled. "Go and see it, Ms Haddad. Go and see it with your own eyes."

— Qu'est-ce qu'il y a dedans ? a demandé Nadia.

— La vérité. » Rosalind faisait tourner sa tasse. « Chaque mois, je prépare les vrais chiffres. Puis je les envoie à Grace. Deux jours plus tard, les chiffres officiels reviennent. Toujours plus gros. Les ventes multipliées par trois.

— Orchid Star ?

— Orchid Star. Silver Bay Trading. Eastern Pearl Logistics. Sept sociétés comme ça. Toutes créées par les gens de Grace. Toutes à Labuan ou aux îles Vierges. » Rosalind a regardé Nadia. « Le vrai Harbourline est une petite compagnie maritime honnête avec trois vieux navires. Il fait environ quatre cents millions de dollars par an. Pas un milliard deux cents. »

Nadia a eu froid, même dans l'air chaud du matin.

« Et l'argent des investisseurs ? Les cinq cents millions de mars ?

— Harbourline doit beaucoup d'argent aux banques. De vieux prêts. Jonathan a besoin du nouvel argent pour payer les vieilles dettes. Puis, l'année prochaine, il lui faudra encore plus d'argent pour payer ces dettes-là. » Rosalind a secoué la tête. « C'est comme un homme qui court de plus en plus vite pour rester au même endroit.

— Pourquoi n'êtes-vous pas allée à la police ? »

Rosalind est restée silencieuse longtemps.

« Je suis veuve. Je m'occupe de ma mère. Elle a quatre-vingt-neuf ans. Si je perds mon travail, je perds tout. Et Jonathan Teo est un homme très puissant.
