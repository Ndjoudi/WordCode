--- EN
In August, Edinburgh changed completely. The Festival arrived, and the city became a giant theatre. There were actors on every corner, musicians in every square, comedians in every pub. Three million visitors came from all over the world.

At the Grand Marlow, every room was full every night. And for the first time in three years, every guest was in the system.

Gregor still worked the night shift. Isobel had asked him to stay until the end of the month. "I need you here," she said. "And I need my brother to believe nothing has changed."

So Gregor sat at the desk every night, polite and quiet. But when the taxi driver with the grey cap came to the door, Gregor shook his head. "Not anymore, Jim. It's finished." The taxi never came back.

The work was crazy. Sami worked fourteen hours a day. Hana took two weeks off from her studies to help at the reception. Rosa's team cleaned more rooms than ever. Callum created a special Festival menu with Scottish salmon and a dessert called "The Royal Mile," with whisky and raspberries. Guests took photos of it before eating.

One evening, a group of young actors from Paris checked in. When they heard Sami's accent, they almost cried with happiness. They gave him free tickets to their show. It was a comedy about a hotel where nothing worked. Sami laughed until his stomach hurt.

At the end of the month, Fiona printed the numbers for August. She put them on the table in front of Sami.

One hundred percent of rooms occupied. The best month in the history of the hotel.

"Douglas receives a copy of this every month," she said. "Automatically, from the system."

"So he knows," said Sami.

"He knows the numbers are excellent. And he knows that's a problem for his plan." Fiona looked at her phone. It was ringing. The name on the screen was "Douglas Crane."

She let it ring three times. Then she answered.
