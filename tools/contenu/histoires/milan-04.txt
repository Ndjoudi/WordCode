--- EN
In October, the production manager, Giulia Conti, called a meeting in the small office next to the atelier.

Her job was to organise the manufacturing of the collection: find the fabrics, buy the buttons, decide which pieces were made where, and make sure everything arrived on time.

"Paris has approved the budget for spring," she said. Her voice was flat. "Thirty percent less than last year."

Aldo put down his pencil. "Thirty percent? With the same collection? Luca wants every stitch by hand."

"I know what Luca wants."

"Then how?"

Giulia looked at her laptop. "Our atelier here can make the show pieces. The forty looks for the runway. But the shops need eight hundred jackets and four hundred coats by March. We can't make those here. Not at this price."

"So who makes them?" asked Chloé.

"A new supplier. Laboratorio Toscana, near Florence. They're fast and very good. And their prices are..." Giulia hesitated. "Very competitive."

"How competitive?" asked Aldo.

Giulia turned her laptop towards them. On the screen, there was a price list.

Chloé looked at the numbers. The price for making one of Luca's hand-finished jackets, from cut fabric to finished piece, was thirty-eight euros.

In the shops, the jacket would sell for two thousand four hundred euros.

"Thirty-eight euros," said Aldo slowly. "For a jacket that takes one of my tailors two days." He took off his glasses. "Giulia, nobody in Italy can pay a skilled tailor for two days with thirty-eight euros. Nobody."

"They have a certificate," said Giulia quickly. "An official audit of working conditions. It's in the file. Everything is legal. Paris checked."

"Who did the audit?"

"An auditing company. They visited in June."

"Did they tell the workshop they were coming?"

Giulia did not answer. She closed her laptop.

"I don't have time for this," she said, standing up. "I have four hundred coats to deliver. If you want to discuss prices, call Paris."

She left. Aldo and Chloé sat in silence.

"Thirty-eight euros," repeated Aldo. "Someone is paying the difference, Chloé. And it's never the customer."

En octobre, la responsable de production, Giulia Conti, a organisé une réunion dans le petit bureau à côté de l'atelier.

Son travail consistait à organiser la fabrication de la collection : trouver les tissus, acheter les boutons, décider quelles pièces étaient faites où, et veiller à ce que tout arrive à temps.

« Paris a validé le budget du printemps, a-t-elle dit. Sa voix était plate. Trente pour cent de moins que l'an dernier. »

Aldo a posé son crayon. « Trente pour cent ? Avec la même collection ? Luca veut chaque point fait main.

— Je sais ce que veut Luca.

— Alors comment ? »

Giulia a regardé son ordinateur. « Notre atelier ici peut faire les pièces du défilé. Les quarante silhouettes. Mais les boutiques ont besoin de huit cents vestes et de quatre cents manteaux d'ici mars. On ne peut pas les faire ici. Pas à ce prix-là.

— Alors qui les fabrique ? a demandé Chloé.

— Un nouveau fournisseur. Laboratorio Toscana, près de Florence. Ils sont rapides et très bons. Et leurs prix sont… » Giulia a hésité. « Très compétitifs.

— Compétitifs comment ? » a demandé Aldo.

Giulia a tourné son ordinateur vers eux. À l'écran, il y avait une liste de prix.

Chloé a regardé les chiffres. Le prix de fabrication d'une des vestes finies main de Luca, du tissu coupé à la pièce terminée, était de trente-huit euros.

En boutique, la veste serait vendue deux mille quatre cents euros.

« Trente-huit euros, a dit Aldo lentement. Pour une veste qui demande deux jours à un de mes tailleurs. » Il a enlevé ses lunettes. « Giulia, personne en Italie ne peut payer un tailleur qualifié pendant deux jours avec trente-huit euros. Personne.
